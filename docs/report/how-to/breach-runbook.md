# Breach runbook: what to do when personal data may have leaked

For the school's Chief Admin and Principal, and for our support team. Plain steps, in order. Under the DPDP Act and Rules, the school (the data fiduciary) tells the **Data Protection Board** and the **people affected** about a personal data breach. We, the processor, tell the school at once.

> Have a lawyer check this runbook and the notification wording before launch.

## 1. In the first hour

1. **Write it down.** Open **Privacy office → Incidents → Record the incident**: what happened, when it was noticed, how bad it looks, roughly how many people. You can correct and add later; the timeline keeps every step.
2. **Stop it getting worse.**
   - A lost or stolen phone or laptop: on **Devices** (or a higher role on the user's page) end every sign-in of that person. Change the password.
   - A shared password or a suspected account takeover: deactivate the account, reset its MFA, end its sign-ins.
   - Data sent to the wrong person: ask them in writing to delete it and confirm.
   - A support grant that should not be open: revoke it (**Support access**).
   - Anything on the server side (an attack, a bug that shows other people's data): call Campus TAB support at once (see the support contact in your contract).
3. **Keep the evidence.** Do not delete records or logs. The audit log cannot be changed by anyone, which is what you want now.

## 2. Within 24 hours

1. Work out **what data** (attendance, marks, fees, contact details, ID numbers, health notes…) and **whose** (how many students, parents, staff). The audit log (**Audit**) shows who saw or changed what, with time, IP and device.
2. Decide the **severity**: high if health data, ID numbers, or data of many children is out; medium for contact details of a class; low if it was contained before anyone saw it.
3. Add each step to the incident's timeline.

## 3. Within 72 hours: tell the Data Protection Board

1. Report the breach to the Data Protection Board of India in the form the Rules require: what happened, when, the data and people affected, what you did, what people should do, and the grievance contact.
2. On the incident, add the step with **Add: Board told**. The deadline on the incident turns from red to done.
3. Keep a copy of what you sent.

## 4. Tell the people affected, without delay

1. Plain words: what happened, what data, what the school is doing, what they can do (for example, be careful with calls asking for fee payments), and whom to contact (the grievance contact from **School settings**).
2. Use the school's usual channels (a notice to the affected sections, email). **Do not** put the leaked data itself in the message.
3. On the incident, **Add: people told**.

## 5. Afterwards

1. Fix the cause (training, a setting, a support ticket for a bug).
2. Close the incident with a last step saying what changed.
3. Review at the next staff meeting. Keep the incident log; it is evidence that the school acts on breaches.

## Our side (Campus TAB support)

- Tell the school's Chief within 24 hours of knowing, with the facts we have, and keep them updated.
- Preserve server logs and backups; do not restore over evidence.
- Help the school answer the Board's questions.
